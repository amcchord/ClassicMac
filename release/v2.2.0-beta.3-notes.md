# ClassicMac 2.2.0 beta 3

ClassicMac 2.2.0 beta 3 is the largest GXMetal compatibility prerelease so far.
It restores Quake III world geometry that regressed in the earlier 2.2.0
betas, fixes Myth II's black battlefield transition, hardens ATI/OpenGL
clipping and process recovery, and turns those failures into permanent focused
regression gates. The ten-game campaign now covers multiple public-RAVE and
Apple OpenGL engines rather than optimizing only for the first two games.

The native Cocoa machine window remains the default. The private browser/VNC
viewer introduced in beta 1 remains available as an optional per-machine
setting.

## Quake III pointer-fan correction

- ATI OpenGL private slot 60 submits pointer-based triangle fans. Its fourth
  argument describes whether the ATI GLD clipped the staged fan; zero is a
  valid ordinary fan, not an instruction to discard the draw.
- GXMetal now renders both zero-marker and nonzero-marker slot-60 fans when
  their bounded vertex count is valid. Invalid counts remain rejected.
- This restores Quake III's missing floors, walls, pedestals, arches, and
  other polygon-shaped world surfaces without changing the protocol or
  relaxing GXMetal's pointer and count validation.
- A silent Q3DM1 route retains five useful human-reviewed gameplay viewpoints
  covering the spawn courtyard, ornate and lit passage arches, walls, floor,
  statues, pedestals, and steps. Cropped near-black-pixel oracles catch the
  former holes while excluding the sky, HUD, and animated content; a named
  frame transition gate also proves the route entered live gameplay.

## Explicit whole-draw OpenGL coordinates

- GXMetal protocol 1.25 adds a negotiated homogeneous-draw capability. A new
  guest marks geometry at the ATI private callback that actually produced it;
  an old host never receives the new flag, and a new host retains a bounded
  compatibility classifier for old guests.
- The host chooses coordinate semantics once for the complete draw instead of
  guessing separately for each vertex. Textured and Gouraud geometry now keep
  signed reciprocal-W and clip-space Z coherently across mixed-sign eye-plane
  triangles, while public RAVE games such as Myth II keep their legacy
  screen-space/no-Z behavior.
- Metal reconstructs clip W for flagged Gouraud draws as well as textured
  draws. Zero and nonfinite reciprocal-W remain malformed, unknown draw bits
  remain rejected, and the new flag is legal only on triangle packets.
- Native protocol and Metal tests cover feature/version identity, valid and
  invalid packet combinations, ordinary flagged rendering, old-guest fallback,
  mixed-sign geometry, far-out clipping, Myth II's exact no-Z terrain case,
  and zero/NaN reciprocal-W rejection.
- The exact packaged guest and signed QEMU repeated all five Q3DM1 views after
  this change. Spawn and passage arches, walls, floor, statues, pedestals,
  weapons, and lighting are complete in all retained frames; cropped
  missing-surface assertions and full-frame transition gates pass, with zero
  renderer fallback.

## Force Quit and relaunch correction

- A new RAVE fragment now starts a fresh GXMetal context/resource generation
  from its first draw-context request. This lets games recover from Mac OS
  Force Quit even when the old fragment could not destroy its host objects.
- QEMU's guest-requested reset atomically clears the render queue and renderer
  while preserving independent cursor and InputSprocket state.
- Generic transport connections are read-only. This prevents an auxiliary
  input/probe client from overwriting a game packet that has been reserved but
  not yet published; the exact two-client interleaving is now a native pipeline
  regression.
- Weekend Warrior passes first title, OS Force Quit, Finder recovery,
  same-boot relaunch, second title, and a ten-second animated-title soak with
  no queue/context fault or renderer fallback.

## Qualification tooling and current status

- The sweep harness can now apply inclusive RGB-range assertions to a stable
  subregion, making visual regression checks more specific than whole-frame
  color counts.
- The harness can also hold and reliably release the primary mouse button.
  That makes classic click-and-hold unit selection, order menus, and graphical
  camera controls scriptable instead of depending on manual timing.
- The checked-in compatibility ledger now records Cro-Mag Rally's accelerated
  Practice/Desert race and steering pass, two current-driver Combat Mission
  Setup-to-Orders rendering passes, and Unreal Tournament's sound-disabled
  live Practice gameplay gate.
- Myth II now reaches a coherent 640x480 “Into the Breach” battlefield. Proven
  ATI private slot-2 calls replace flags-zero, single-level base images in
  place without changing the existing texture's format, dimensions, or
  identity; slot 4
  returns the synchronous `TQADevice` draw-buffer view the game consumes, and
  no-Z, no-fog textured draws no longer reject finite signed eye depth that is
  unused by the pipeline. The exact signed-candidate silent gate records direct
  presentation, balanced frame boundaries, 25.74-26.59 battlefield fps, and
  zero transport/resource rejects.
- The in-guest conformance app covers the exact private update/readback calls,
  including repeated readback and subsequent public draw-buffer access. Host
  Metal regressions retain Myth's exact 224-byte no-Z textured fan,
  preserve normalized textured fog depth, reject the out-of-range value when
  depth or fog consumes it, and prove old-draw/upload/new-draw ordering.
- Dark Vengeance and HAVOC remain pre-3D application/media blockers. Those
  results are documented rather than hidden by the current graphics fixes.

## Unreal Tournament CFM layout correction

- Unreal Tournament v348 caught a guest-driver layout regression before its
  first RAVE context: the initial combined Myth II slot-2/slot-4 build stopped
  engine discovery after the three identity Gestalts even though neither new
  callback had executed.
- Controlled builds with slot 2 alone, slot 4 alone, and both callbacks as
  no-ops all reach the accelerated menu. Keeping both real callbacks while
  size-optimizing only the cold slot-2 update path also passes, with 21,367
  direct frames and zero presentation fallback in the retained menu gate.
- The PowerPC build now audits the last known-good CFM import-glue layout, so
  later code growth fails clearly at build time until the new layout has been
  qualified in Mac OS 9 rather than resurfacing as a game-only black screen.

## Unreal Tournament homogeneous clipping correction

- UT's later `Out of RAVE texture memory` dialogs were not capacity failures.
  QEMU's retained queue diagnostics localized both to otherwise valid
  ATI-private textured fans that GXMetal rejected as malformed geometry.
- Apple's ATI OpenGL bridge submits finite transformed geometry outside the
  normalized clip volume. One exact four-vertex fan is wholly behind the eye
  with negative reciprocal-W and Z above one; the next is beyond the far plane
  with positive reciprocal-W and negative Z. Native hardware clips these
  primitives instead of treating them as fatal driver input.
- GXMetal now preserves signed reciprocal-W and out-of-range Z only for the
  ATI-private, non-perspective-Z path and reconstructs signed homogeneous clip
  coordinates for Metal. Public RAVE validation stays strict, and zero or
  nonfinite reciprocal-W remains rejected.
- Host regressions replay both exact UT fans, prove that they leave the target
  untouched, and exercise a mixed-sign fan whose visible portion must survive
  eye-plane clipping.
- The final muted Tempest replay proves live world, weapon and HUD rendering,
  forward movement, left/right turning, firing, damage, death, and respawn.
  Forty profiles contain 9,472 direct frames with zero fallback at
  80.36-131.59 fps; the trace has no queue fault, texture error, or rejected
  geometry.

This remains a prerelease, not a claim that every RAVE or OpenGL game is
compatible. Bugdom, Future Cop, Weekend Warrior, Cro-Mag Rally, Combat Mission,
Myth II, Oni, Quake III, and Unreal Tournament have useful accelerated evidence
at different qualification depths; Dark Vengeance and HAVOC remain documented
pre-RAVE application/media blockers. Several titles still need some
combination of longer gameplay, clean quit/relaunch, and audio coverage. See the
[current ten-game compatibility ledger](https://github.com/amcchord/ClassicMac/blob/v2.2.0-beta.3/docs/gxmetal-ten-game-compatibility.md)
and
[scripted sweep guide](https://github.com/amcchord/ClassicMac/blob/v2.2.0-beta.3/docs/gxmetal-game-sweep.md)
for the evidence-backed matrix and reproduction procedures.

artifact hashes and Apple notarization identifiers appear below after the
