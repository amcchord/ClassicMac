# ClassicMac 2.2.1

ClassicMac 2.2.1 is the stable release of the GXMetal and viewer work developed
through the 2.2 beta series. It substantially broadens Mac OS 9 RAVE and Apple
OpenGL compatibility, adds reproducible game qualification tooling, restores
Quake III and Myth II regressions found during beta testing, and keeps the
normal native Mac VM window as the default.

## Native window and optional browser viewer

- New and existing machines open in the native QEMU Cocoa window by default.
- **View in browser (VNC)** remains an optional per-machine setting during
  creation and under Viewer settings. It takes effect on the next start.
- Native-window machines do not start the VNC, WebSocket, or browser server.
  Browser-viewed machines use private Unix and loopback-only endpoints.
- The browser viewer retains sharp integer scaling, fullscreen, sticky classic
  modifiers, Escape, and relative game-mouse capture for automation or remote
  interaction.

## GXMetal 2.2.1

- GXMetal carries QuickDraw 3D RAVE and Apple's classic ATI/OpenGL geometry,
  texture, depth, blending, fog, clipping, readback, and presentation work to
  the host Metal renderer while retaining Apple Software RAVE as fallback.
- Protocol 1.25 adds negotiated whole-draw homogeneous-coordinate provenance.
  New guests identify triangles at the ATI private callback that produced them,
  so textured and Gouraud geometry use signed reciprocal-W and clip-space Z
  consistently across the complete draw. New hosts retain a bounded fallback
  for old guests; public RAVE validation remains strict.
- Independent OpenGL MIN, MAG, and mip filtering is preserved for both texture
  units. Draw-buffer readback/writeback uses validated dirty rectangles and
  keeps display gamma, synchronization, and untouched pixels correct.
- ATI private triangle lists, strips, fans, quads, quad strips, polygons,
  clipped pointer fans, multitexture state, alpha/depth/blend/fog state, clear,
  swap, and context ownership now follow the observed Apple driver ABIs.
- Complex QuickDraw regions, deep-Z contexts, RGB24 and classic intensity
  formats, channel/depth write masks, chromakey, alpha testing, large meshes,
  dynamic textures, draw-buffer access, and process-generation reset all have
  native regression coverage.

## Game corrections

- Quake III's zero-marker pointer fans are valid world geometry. Restoring
  them fixes the missing floors, walls, pedestals, arches, and other
  polygon-shaped holes reported in the early 2.2 betas.
- The final protocol-1.25 candidate passes five human-reviewed Q3DM1 views:
  spawn courtyard and arches, left courtyard, right arches/statue, forward
  courtyard detail, and the forward-right passage. Cropped missing-surface
  thresholds and camera-transition gates pass with direct presentation and
  zero fallback.
- Myth II's public-RAVE, no-depth/no-fog terrain remains on its legacy
  screen-space depth path instead of being misclassified as ATI/OpenGL clip
  geometry. Its battlefield and effects-heavy film render coherently.
- A fresh rendering process now resets stale context/resource generations,
  allowing games to recover after Force Quit. Independent input/probe clients
  cannot overwrite an unpublished RAVE packet.
- Unreal Tournament's private OpenGL bridge can submit finite geometry behind
  the eye or beyond a clip plane. GXMetal clips those draws instead of
  reporting a misleading texture-memory failure, and the guest build audits
  its UT-sensitive CFM layout.

## Compatibility campaign

The checked-in ten-game campaign uses immutable source images, isolated
copy-on-write clones, deterministic VNC input, named screenshots, semantic
pixel/frame assertions, rolling renderer profiles, matched software controls,
and exact artifact hashes. The evidence supports these qualified states:

- Bugdom, Future Cop: LAPD, Combat Mission, Weekend Warrior, and Cro-Mag Rally
  have representative accelerated public-RAVE gameplay and input evidence.
- Quake III has the exact five-view world-geometry regression described above.
- Myth II has coherent battlefield/effects evidence and partial gameplay/input
  coverage.
- Oni has coherent warehouse gameplay, relative look, movement, action, F1
  Comlink/resume, and a clean first-process Quit/Yes return to Finder.
- Unreal Tournament reaches coherent sound-disabled Tempest gameplay with
  movement and combat. A later same-process visible-progress/quit lifecycle
  gap remains documented.
- Dark Vengeance and HAVOC remain pre-RAVE application/media/runtime blockers;
  neither reaches a GXMetal context in the retained controls.

This is substantially broader evidence than the original two-game test set,
but it is not a claim that every classic RAVE or OpenGL title is compatible.
Audio and longer lifecycle coverage remain separate qualification dimensions.
See the
[ten-game compatibility ledger](https://github.com/amcchord/ClassicMac/blob/v2.2.1/docs/gxmetal-ten-game-compatibility.md)
and
[scripted sweep guide](https://github.com/amcchord/ClassicMac/blob/v2.2.1/docs/gxmetal-game-sweep.md)
for the evidence and reproduction procedures.

## Validation

- Complete GXMetal protocol, queue, guest transport, renderer, dirty tracking,
  pipeline, ATI compatibility, RAVE compatibility, AGL probe, Metal,
  VNC-harness, diagnostics, and PEF suites.
- Warning-as-error PowerPC guest build, PEF initialization checks, and the
  Unreal Tournament CFM-layout gate.
- Clean QEMU/NDRV rebuild and GXMetal transport/input self-checks.
- 39 Swift app tests covering native-window defaults, optional VNC, migration,
  and both machine launch contracts.
- Developer ID signing, hardened runtime, Apple notarization and stapling,
  mounted-DMG verification, and Gatekeeper assessment.
